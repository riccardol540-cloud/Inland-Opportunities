# Research brief — EUNIC Spaces of Culture 2026 (Sub-Saharan Africa)

- **Title**: Spaces of Culture 2026 — call for cultural relations projects in Sub-Saharan Africa
- **Funder / host**: EUNIC (European Union National Institutes for Culture), with the European Commission / EEAS and EU Delegations
- **Channel**: partnership (multilateral cultural-relations consortium)
- **Link**: https://eunic.eu/news/spaces-of-culture-2026
- **Deadline**: **21 June 2026** (23:59 CAT) — applications to spacesofculture@eunicglobal.eu. Partners are expected to align on concept and partnership **before 30 April 2026**. Implementation window **1 Sep 2026 – 31 Aug 2027**.
- **Budget / award size**: grants **up to €50,000** per project; minimum **5% co-funding** from the partners.
- **Eligibility**: projects must be built as a **triangular partnership** — at least **three local cultural / civil-society partners** in the target country, at least **three full EUNIC members** (two where no cluster exists), and involvement of an **EU Delegation**. INLAND cannot lead (leads are national institutes), but can enter as the **European cultural partner** inside a EUNIC-member-led cluster.

## Fit score: **3 / 5**

**Rationale**: Editorially and territorially this is a strong match — it funds **Africa–Europe cultural relations** with a meaningful production budget (€50k), and INLAND already has **two in-progress African editions (Rwanda, Angola)** plus standing relationships with the exact institutes that lead these clusters (Goethe-Institut, Institut français are both live INLAND funding targets). It is held to a 3, not higher, purely by **structure**: INLAND must be recruited into a consortium it cannot lead, requiring a EUNIC cluster, three local partners and an EU Delegation to be assembled around the project. High strategic value, high coordination cost.

## Possible territory / angle (per research-angle.md)

Pair with an **in-progress African edition** where a EUNIC cluster is active and the INLAND angle is already researched:

- **Rwanda** — the "what survived" frame from the Goethe Africa–Europe work (endangered *inanga* zither masters; foot-crushed banana beer *urwagwa* as social infrastructure; the Gishwati resettlement afterlife). A Kigali EUNIC cluster (Goethe, Institut français, and others) plus named local custodian-partners and the EU Delegation would satisfy the triangular structure.
- **Angola** — the Lusophone edition already drafted ("The Fire That Stays Lit"); a Luanda cluster could anchor a cultural-relations project around named custodians and locatable places.

The cultural-relations framing (fair, co-created partnership; not a one-way commission) fits INLAND's *listening, not extraction* method. Loud story stays background; lens is continuity.
